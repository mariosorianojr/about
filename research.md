My group's research is grounded in hydrology and draws on theoretical frameworks and methodologies from sustainability science and applied data science. We are interested in questions that revolve around the complex interlinkages surrounding water as a natural resource and a natural hazard. We employ high fidelity hydrological modeling, geospatial analysis, machine learning, and various field data collection and stakeholder engagement techniques to probe questions about water quantity and quality from a human-environment systems perspective.

<p align="left"> 
We are developing interdisciplinary, mechanistic frameworks that leverage hydrological models and data science methods to examine the impact of human activities on groundwater quality, public health, and environmental justice. A major focus has been on unconventional oil and gas development in the Appalachian Basin (USA), with project involvements including the <a href="https://medicine.yale.edu/lab/deziel/projects/drinking-water/appalachia/" title="water">Yale Water & Energy Resources Study</a> and <a href="https://www.heienergy.org/research/water-quality/saiers" title="hei">A Groundwater Modeling Framework for Elucidating Community Exposures Across the Marcellus Region to Contamination Associated with Oil and Gas Development</a>.
</p>
<br /><br />
<p align="center">

<p align="left"> 
Hydrologic processes have traditionally been modeled using physically based approaches that utilize the numerical solution of differential equations. One challenge with such physically based modeling is that as the models expand into larger spatial scales or higher resolutions, the computational requirements can rapidly become intractable. We are investigating the application, performance, and transferability of scientific machine learning approaches to overcome this computational challenge across applications ranging from pore to continental scales. Collaborators include the <a href="http://maxwell.princeton.edu/" title="igwmc">Integrated GroundWater Modeling Center</a> and the <a href="https://pinzhang3.github.io/about/" title="zhanglab">Computational Granular Mechanics Lab</a>.
</p>
<br /><br />
<p align="center">

<p align="left"> 
Groundwater accounts for the largest volume of accessible freshwater on Earth, and it supplies drinking water to over two billion people and supports half of global irrigation for agriculture. We aim to advance sustainable groundwater management in Southeast and South Asia, where rapid urbanization, increasing demand, and climate impacts pose compounding pressures on this precious resource. An ongoing project examines the spatiotemporal patterns and drivers of irrigation well expansion in Odisha (eastern India), alongside its potential consequences for long-term groundwater availability, agricultural productivity, and human well-being.
</p>

<p align="left"> 
We are investigating coastal risks and community resilience from multiple lenses: remote sensing, modeling, and storytelling. Our podcast series, <a href="https://bluelabmedia.org/projects/podcasts/carried-by-water/" title="bluelab"><i>Carried by Water</i></a>, explores stories on evolving ideas of home and future in the face of increasing coastal disasters. The podcast won the <a href="https://bluelabmedia.org/news-category/announcements/blue-lab-podcast-carried-by-water-wins-the-njafm-media-award/" title="njafm">2025 NJAFM Media Award</a> for science communication/ documentary.
</p>
