My group's research is grounded in hydrology and draws on theoretical frameworks and methodologies from sustainability science and applied data science. We are interested in questions that revolve around the complex interlinkages surrounding water as a natural resource and a natural hazard. We employ high fidelity hydrological modeling, geospatial analysis, machine learning, and various field data collection and stakeholder engagement techniques to probe questions about water quantity and quality from a human-environment systems perspective.
<br /><br /><br />
<p align="center">
  <img src="india.png" alt="india" />
  <br /><br />Groundwater-Energy Nexus in Agricultural Irrigation Expansion
</p>
<p align="left"> 
India is the world’s largest user of groundwater for agriculture. Large parts of the country’s northwest exhibit severe depletion, driven by various energy policies that enabled unconstrained pumping. In areas targeted for new irrigation expansion, such as eastern India, energy policies may either reproduce the same patterns or chart out more sustainable trajectories. This project aims to describe spatiotemporal patterns of well expansion in Odisha (eastern India), to characterize such expansion’s drivers and consequences, and evaluate the potential role of energy policy in shaping long-term groundwater availability, agricultural productivity, and socioeconomic outcomes.
</p>

<p align="left"> 
We are developing interdisciplinary, mechanistic frameworks that leverage hydrological models and data science approaches to examine the impact of unconventional energy development on groundwater quality, public health, and environmental justice in the Appalachian Basin (USA). Projects include the <a href="https://medicine.yale.edu/lab/deziel/projects/drinking-water/appalachia/" title="water">Yale Water & Energy Resources Study</a> and <a href="https://www.heienergy.org/research/water-quality/saiers" title="hei">A Groundwater Modeling Framework for Elucidating Community Exposures Across the Marcellus Region to Contamination Associated with Oil and Gas Development</a>.
</p>
<br /><br />
<p align="center">
  <img src="colorado.png" alt="colorado" />
  <br /><br />Scientific Machine Learning Approaches for Modeling Hydrologic Processes
</p>
<p align="left"> 
Hydrologic processes have traditionally been modeled using physically based approaches that utilize the numerical solution of differential equations. One challenge with such physically based modeling is that as the models expand into larger spatial scales or higher resolutions, the computational requirements can rapidly become intractable. We are investigating the application, performance, and transferability of scientific machine learning approaches to overcome this computational challenge across applications ranging from pore to continental scales. Collaborators include the <a href="http://maxwell.princeton.edu/" title="igwmc">Integrated GroundWater Modeling Center</a> and the <a href="https://pinzhang3.github.io/about/" title="zhanglab">Computational Granular Mechanics Lab</a>.
</p>

<p align="left"> 
We are investigating coastal hazards from multiple lenses: remote sensing, modeling, and storytelling. Our podcast series, <a href="https://bluelabmedia.org/projects/podcasts/carried-by-water/" title="bluelab"><i>Carried by Water</i></a>, explores the role of storytelling in communicating key lessons learned from scholarly research and lived experiences of coastal disasters. The podcast won the <a href="https://bluelabmedia.org/news-category/announcements/blue-lab-podcast-carried-by-water-wins-the-njafm-media-award/" title="njafm">2025 NJAFM Media Award</a> for science communication/ documentary.
</p>
